Sewage treatment plant (STP)

A sewage treatment plant is a system that treats wastewater from residential, commercial, institutional and industrial sources. It removes pollutants so the treated water can be safely discharged into the environment or reused.

How does an STP work?

Wastewater moves through seven stages, each removing more of what shouldn't reach a river, lake or reuse system.

  1. Wastewater in

    Wastewater flows into the treatment system.

  2. Equalization tank

    Balances the flow and quality of the incoming wastewater.

  3. Biological treatment

    Microorganisms break down organic matter and pollutants.

  4. Clarification

    Solids are separated from the treated water.

  5. Filtration

    Further removes fine particles for clearer water.

  6. Disinfection

    Kills harmful pathogens through a disinfection process.

  7. Safe discharge or water reuse

    The treated water is now safe to be discharged or reused.

Why do businesses need an STP?

An STP protects the environment around your site, keeps you within the law and supports a healthier community.

Environmental responsibility

Helps protect our rivers, lakes and groundwater.

Legal compliance

Meets DENR and, where applicable, LLDA regulations.

Promotes sustainability

Supports a cleaner environment and better community health.

Get a permit from DENR and LLDA

You are required to secure a discharge permit from DENR and, where applicable, LLDA before releasing treated water into the environment. We can help you through the process.

DENR

Department of Environment and Natural Resources

Sets the standards for wastewater discharge to protect water quality and the environment.

LLDA

For facilities within the Laguna Lake Region

Laguna Lake Development Authority

Regulates discharges within the Laguna de Bay watershed to preserve the lake and its surrounding communities.
